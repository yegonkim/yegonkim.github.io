
- $\pi^\ast$ is the AIXI policy.
- We want to see if training a student on short horizon trajectories generated by $\pi^\ast$ will lead to a student policy that generalizes to longer horizons.

Simplest case:
- Sample independent $a^1_{1:t}, \dots, a^N_{1:t} \sim \pi^\ast$ for some small $t$.
- $w(\pi_i \mid a^1_{1:t}, \dots, a^N_{1:t}) \propto w(\pi_i) \pi_i (a^1_{1:t}) \cdots \pi_i (a^N_{1:t}) $
- $\zeta_N = \sum_i w(\pi_i \mid a^1_{1:t}, \dots, a^N_{1:t}) \pi_i$ is the student policy.
- As $N \to \infty$, $\zeta_N \to \zeta$.
- Can we show convergence in TV of $\zeta$ to $\pi^\ast$ as $t \to \infty$?
- Can we then show convergence of $\zeta$ to optimality in terms of the value function $V^\zeta ( \cdot \mid h_{<t} )$?

Convergence rate:
- In general, convergence of $\zeta$ to $\pi^\ast$ can be very slow. We can have a large mass of hypotheses that are consistent with $\pi^\ast$ only up to time $t$, and then sharply deviate.
- Deviating from $\pi^\ast$ at time $t$ incurs a complexity cost of roughly $K(t)$, which goes to infinity as $t \to \infty$. So policies that make a sharp deviation at time $t$ will have a prior weight of at most $2^{-K(t)}$.
- More generally, the behavior of stopping complexity would be relevant.
- Under the simplicity prior, can we show some convergence rate of $\zeta$ to $\pi^\ast$?

Considering sample size $N$:
- Let's consider the rate of convergence w.r.t. $N$.
- Suppose $t$ is sampled from some distribution, say the exponential distribution.
- How fast does $\zeta$ converge to $\pi^\ast$ as $N \to \infty$?

Unrealizability:
- How do the above questions change if $\pi^\ast$ is not in the hypothesis class? e.g. AIXI is not computable.
- Although $\pi^\ast$ is not computable, $\pi^\ast_{1:t}$ is computable.How hard is it to describe $\pi^\ast_{1:t}$ without a halting oracle? i.e. What is $K(\pi^\ast_{1:t})$ as a function of $t$? 

Reinforcement learning:
- Above, we considered supervised learning with AIXI as the teacher, because it's simpler to think about than RL. The only difference with RL is that RL doesn't dictate how to tie-break actions.
- How do the above questions change if we use RL instead of supervised learning?
- e.g. In the simplest case, we can have an environment $\mu$, and we define $w_t (\pi_i) = w(\pi_i) \mathbb I [ \pi_i \in \argmax_\pi V^\pi_{\mu_{1:t}}]$ with finite horizon $t$.
